That's a neat chain reaction! When you start pulling on that thread, you realize that this is not about an individual phone, but about a domino effect that spreads all the way from your pocket and out into the social structure.

Classic system effect when you break it down step by step:

Primary effect (Device): Consumption is drastically reduced, charging intervals jump up to 12–14 days, and the lithium-ion battery's life cycle is doubled (or more) because it avoids deep discharges and constant heat generation from background jobs.

Secondary effect (Consumer): Drastically reduced e-case because the hardware and battery last twice as long, plus you don't have to worry about power banks and broken charging connectors.

Tertiary effect (Infrastructure): The operators and cloud giants don't have to drown their masts, routers and data centers in terabytes of pointless background chatter.  It cuts down on huge amounts of electricity consumption, reduces wear and tear on network components, and postpones the need for expensive base station expansion.

It's pretty absurd to think that this entire cascade of profits is being blocked by software that's basically just there to send telemetry and keep unnecessary cloud connections running. To show that you can turn that around without even touching the basic functions is a pretty heavy slap in the face to the industry!
